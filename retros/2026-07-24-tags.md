### Rationale (one line each)

- **#1** — 4/4 is not statistical noise: every permission claim from a sub-agent needed correction, because the sub-agent reads local syntax without the driver's accumulated framework model. The token-check (§11) is necessary but not sufficient for this class of claim; a semantic-verification step is the missing gate.
- **#2** — The §11 ratio binary (evidence vs design/applied) produces a false exhaustion signal for absence-centred blocks, or requires the driver to improvise a workaround; naming the type makes the ratio interpretation unambiguous and reproducible across drivers.
- **#3** — A vendor guide documents intent; only the decompile reveals failure modes. §269.5's "what the doc does not resolve" was invented ad hoc and proved immediately valuable; standardizing it makes doc-synthesis blocks more honest and more useful to a future reader.
- **#4** — The rule exists; it fired again on a second target despite being in the kit. Prose alone is insufficient when the violation originates inside a delegated sub-agent the driver cannot intercept mid-commit. Mechanization at step 6 (before the next block) closes the window.
- **#5** — The AUDIT-FIRST approach in step e reduces wrong premises but does not name the refutable-premise risk; a driver inheriting a prior block's framing (as happened with T6/T7) has no prompt to dependency-check that assumption. A closing sentence in step e addresses this at the moment the backlog is seeded.

## Honest verdict

Two of the five deltas are STRUCTURAL — they address failure modes that will recur in any focus with delegated sweeps over a framework-heavy codebase (delta #1: security-semantic mismatch; delta #4: two-block commit from a multi-gap sub-agent). Both were caught by existing mechanisms (driver token-check; archive.sh gate), but the catches were late and ad hoc. Making them mechanical is worth the delta.

Delta #2 (absence-centred block type) and delta #5 (refuted premise) are both cases where the driver improvised correctly but the kit provides no vocabulary. Naming the type and the practice prevents the next driver from having to re-invent them.

Delta #3 (what the doc does not resolve) is the lightest — a documentation convention the driver invented and immediately found valuable, proposed for standardization. It can be optional.

The eight already-covered items confirm the core machinery (token-check, e3 scout, back-fill, proven-absence closure, sub-agent scope validation) is sound. The fabricated-cite was caught mid-run, not at STOP. The focus delivered a well-structured corpus despite the issues. The kit gaps here are at the margins, not the foundation.
