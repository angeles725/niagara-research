For each delta above, one line of rationale (WHY it matters, what it costs, expected impact):

- **#1** — Without naming this, the next focus-level closer either skips a valuable consolidation (worse corpus) or writes one while second-guessing whether it's "against the rules" (friction). Cost: a few lines in §8/PROMPT-LOOP. Impact: makes a proven-good improvisation repeatable and removes ambiguity about when synthesis blocks are allowed.
- **#2** — Without a named remittance-closure pattern, the next operator under time/context pressure either pads a thin gap with a low-value block (corpus bloat) or silently drops it without the citation discipline B155 used. Cost: one paragraph + evidence-bar bullet. Impact: legitimizes and standardizes an efficient, already-proven closure path.
- **#3** — Tier declaration exists only in an ephemeral chat report today; it evaporates once the session ends, so no future audit (including this retro) can check compliance except by inference. Cost: one table column. Impact: makes the MODEL TIER heuristic auditable, not just aspirational.
- **#4** — The citation convention for beautified temps is currently reinvented prose per block (already duplicated near-verbatim in B153 and B155). Cost: a short paragraph in §5/§10. Impact: any future minified-JS/obfuscated-code gap reuses a settled convention instead of re-deriving it, and reviewers know exactly how to trust a `file:line` that points at a non-committed temp file.
- **#5** — Low cost (a definitional split in one paragraph), but prevents a real reputational/traceability risk: mislabeling a correct-for-its-artifact claim as a "correction" can read as "B50 was wrong" when it wasn't — it described a different tree entirely.
- **#6** — Cheap (one `.gitignore` line + a sentence), but the repo's own history shows this bites twice already; a target-level template fix removes the recurring manual vigilance the operator currently has to apply on every commit.
- **#7** — Cheapest fix (one sentence in the template) for a real but modest hygiene drift; the process-tip half (re-Read before Edit near heavy Unicode) is offered as a mitigation, not asserted as a proven root cause, since the tool-failure claim itself isn't independently verifiable from the corpus.

## Honest verdict

This run genuinely surfaced new material for the kit — it is not a "nothing new" retro. The two sharpest findings
(#1 focus-closing synthesis blocks, #2 closure-by-remittance) are structural: both are patterns the operator used
correctly and productively, but neither is named anywhere in METHODOLOGY.md or PROMPT-LOOP.md, and both will recur
on the next multi-block focus (naming them now makes the next operator's judgment call cheaper and more consistent).
The remaining five deltas are real but smaller: one taxonomy refinement (#5), one durable-audit-trail gap for an
existing rule that IS already being followed in spirit but not persisted (#3), one citation convention that exists
in practice (duplicated across two block headers) but not in the kit (#4), and two low-cost hygiene notes (#6, #7)
where the run's own artifacts show the friction (accidental `.atl` commits earlier in this repo's history; a
3-bullet "Last iteration" drift) without fully confirming the operator's specific "Edit tool vs. Unicode" root-cause
narrative — that part is flagged as plausible-but-unverified, not asserted as fact. Nothing in this run contradicted
an existing kit rule; §11's ratio guidance and §16's multi-focus mechanics were both applied correctly and are
listed under "Already covered" rather than re-proposed.
