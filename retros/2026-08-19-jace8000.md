Rationale (WHY · cost · impact):

- **#1** — The kit's re-measure discipline is worded for NEGATIVES only; this run's near-miss was a dramatic POSITIVE (a false TLS-downgrade "vulnerability") that would have driven a wrong client security escalation. `FALSIFY BEFORE REPORTING` (PROMPT-LOOP) partly covers the escalation angle, but neither rule names the specific hazard: a probe tool's own status line (`CONNECTED`, a banner, a handshake-open message) is emitted on TCP/transport events, not on protocol acceptance, and "client cannot offer version X" is indistinguishable at a glance from "server refused version X". Cost: two sentences. Impact: closes a symmetric blind spot and a reusable live-probe trap for any TLS/protocol sweep.
- **#2** — Recurred across the run and cost a workaround on every Bash call (and again in this retro). Cost: one operational note in the WSL bring-up doc. Impact: removes a repeated friction that silently breaks `rg`/`fd`/pipelines the kit assumes are on PATH. Env-scoped (WSL), hence MEDIUM not HIGH.
- **#3** — Pure wording: the methodology already says n/a is expected for a synthesis block, but the SCRIPT's WARN still tells the author to "add file:line citations", which is wrong advice for a legitimately `file:line`-free block. Cost: one message string. Impact: stops a correct block from looking broken to its author.
